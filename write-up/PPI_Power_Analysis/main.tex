\begin{abstract}
Modern studies increasingly rely on predictive models trained on large
unlabeled datasets, yet classical power and sample-size calculations
assume only labeled data contribute to statistical precision.  Recent
advances in \emph{Prediction-Powered Inference} (PPI) have shown that
model predictions can be incorporated into estimation to yield unbiased,
label-efficient inference, but existing work focuses on characterizing
inferential gains rather than the practical question: \emph{how many
samples need to be labeled} to achieve desired study power?

We present \texttt{PPPower}, a framework for prediction-powered power
and sample-size calculation.  Building on PPI and semiparametric
efficiency theory, \texttt{PPPower} provides closed-form and
simulation-based formulas for statistical power and required labeled
sample sizes.  The framework accepts familiar predictive-performance
metrics---$R^2$, sensitivity/specificity, or confusion matrices---making
it readily usable with standard model-validation outputs.  A
comprehensive simulation study spanning 13~settings validates all
analytical formulas, confirms Type~I error calibration, verifies sample
size inversion, and demonstrates convergence of the plug-in~$\lambda$.
Our R package is available at
\texttt{https://github.com/yiqunchen/pppower}.
\end{abstract}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Modern data analysis is undergoing a \emph{prediction-powered
transition}.  Scientists have access to massive covariate datasets that
are easy to collect, but high-quality labels remain costly and
time-consuming to obtain.  A natural idea is to leverage ML/AI
predictions on unlabeled data for statistical inference; however,
imperfect models may yield biased predictions, and using them directly
can lead to misleading conclusions.

Recent work has addressed this challenge through \emph{Prediction-Powered
Inference} (PPI)~\citep{angelopoulos2023prediction} and
extensions~\citep{miao2025assumption,wang2020methods,zrnic2024cross},
providing principled recipes for unbiased, efficient inference that
combines a small labeled dataset with a large pool of predictions.
While PPI and its tuned variant PPI++ offer principled gains in label
efficiency, their practical adoption requires one additional ingredient:
a framework for \emph{power and sample-size calculation}.

\begin{tcolorbox}
\emph{Given the predictive model performance, are the available labeled
and unlabeled samples sufficient to detect an effect of interest with
desired power?}
\end{tcolorbox}

We close this gap by introducing \texttt{PPPower}, a rigorous framework
for prediction-powered power analysis accompanied by a companion R
package.  This note covers mean and proportion settings---including
paired designs, two-sample comparisons, and binary surrogates such as
LLM-as-a-judge scores.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:notation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Consider i.i.d.\ labeled data $\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ drawn from
$P_{XY}$ on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$, and an independent pool of
unlabeled covariates $\{\wt X_j\}_{j=1}^{N}$ from $P_X$.  A
pre-trained predictor $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}$ yields predictions
$f_i = f(X_i)$ and $\wt f_j = f(\wt X_j)$.

We use $\rho_{Yf} = \Cov(Y,f)/(\sigma_Y\sigma_f)$ for the correlation
between $Y$ and $f(X)$ throughout, with $\sigma_Y^2 = \Var(Y)$ and
$\sigma_f^2 = \Var(f(X))$.  The parameter of interest is
$\theta^\star = \E[Y]$, and we test $H_0:\theta = \theta_0$ versus
$H_1:\theta = \theta_0 + \Delta$ at two-sided level~$\alpha$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Classical Power Analysis}
\label{sec:classical}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The classical sample mean $\bar Y = n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i$ has
variance $\sigma_Y^2/n$.  The power of the two-sided Wald test is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-power}
\text{Power} = \Phi\!\left(-z_{1-\alpha/2} +
  \frac{|\Delta|}{\sigma_Y/\sqrt{n}}\right) +
  \Phi\!\left(-z_{1-\alpha/2} -
  \frac{|\Delta|}{\sigma_Y/\sqrt{n}}\right).
\end{equation}
To achieve target power $1-\beta$, the required sample size is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-n}
n_{\text{classical}} \ge
  \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{\bigl(\Delta/(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})\bigr)^2}.
\end{equation}
For two independent groups with equal variances and balanced design, the
per-group requirement doubles:
$n \ge 2\sigma_Y^2 / (\Delta/(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}))^2$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Prediction-Powered Power Analysis}
\label{sec:ppi-power}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We show how auxiliary predictions can dramatically reduce labeled sample
requirements.  The key insight is that a good predictor absorbs much of
the variance in~$Y$, leaving only the residual variance to be estimated
from labeled data.

\subsection{The PPI Estimator}
\label{sec:ppi-vanilla}

The population mean decomposes as
$\theta^\star = \E[f(X)] + \E[Y - f(X)]$.
The PPI estimator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi-estimator}
\wh\theta_{\mathrm{PPI}} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N
  \wt f_j}_{\text{unlabeled}} +
  \underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (Y_i - f_i)}_{\text{labeled
  correction}},
\end{equation}
with variance
$\Var(\wh\theta_{\mathrm{PPI}}) = \sigma_f^2/N +
\sigma_\varepsilon^2/n$, where
$\sigma_\varepsilon^2 = \Var(Y - f(X))$.

\subsection{The PPI++ Estimator}
\label{sec:ppi++}

PPI++~\citep{angelopoulos2023ppipp} introduces a tunable blend weight:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-estimator}
\wh\theta_\lambda = \bar{Y}_L + \lambda(\bar{f}_U - \bar{f}_L),
\end{equation}
with variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-variance}
\Var(\wh\theta_\lambda) = \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{n} +
  \lambda^2 \sigma_f^2 \Bigl(\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{n}\Bigr) -
  \frac{2\lambda\,\Cov(Y,f)}{n}.
\end{equation}
Minimizing over $\lambda$ yields the oracle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-star}
\lambda^\star = \frac{\Cov(Y,f)}{(1 + n/N)\,\sigma_f^2},
\end{equation}
with optimal variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-var-opt}
\Var(\wh\theta_{\lambda^\star}) = \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{n} -
  \frac{\Cov(Y,f)^2}{\sigma_f^2}\cdot\frac{N}{n(n+N)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Connection to Semiparametric Efficiency}
\label{sec:eif}

The PPI++ estimator with $\lambda^\star$ achieves the semiparametric
efficiency bound.  The optimal augmented influence function is
\[
\psi_{\lambda^\star}(Y,f) = Y - \theta^\star -
  \frac{\Cov(Y,f)}{\sigma_f^2}(f - \E[f]),
\]
with variance $\sigma_Y^2(1 - \rho_{Yf}^2)$.

\subsection{Power Formula for PPI++}
\label{sec:ppi-power-formula}

Using~\eqref{eq:ppi++-var-opt}, the power for testing
$H_0:\theta=\theta_0$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-power}
\text{Power} = \Phi\!\left(-z_{1-\alpha/2} +
  \frac{|\Delta|}{\sqrt{\Var(\wh\theta_{\lambda^\star})}}\right) +
  \Phi\!\left(-z_{1-\alpha/2} -
  \frac{|\Delta|}{\sqrt{\Var(\wh\theta_{\lambda^\star})}}\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sample Size Inversion}
\label{sec:n-inversion}

Setting $\Var(\wh\theta_{\lambda^\star}) \le S^2$, where
$S^2 = (\Delta/(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}))^2$, and
substituting~\eqref{eq:ppi++-var-opt} yields a quadratic in~$n$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-n-formula}
n \ge \frac{\sigma_Y^2 - S^2 N +
  \sqrt{(\sigma_Y^2 - S^2 N)^2 + 4S^2 N\sigma_Y^2(1 - \rho_{Yf}^2)}}
  {2S^2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Rule of Thumb: $n_{\text{PPI++}}/n_{\text{classical}}
  \approx 1 - R^2$}
\label{sec:rule-of-thumb}

In the regime $N \gg n$, the PPI++ variance simplifies to
$\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho_{Yf}^2)/n$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule-of-thumb}
\frac{n_{\text{PPI++}}}{n_{\text{classical}}} \approx 1 - \rho_{Yf}^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{tcolorbox}
\textbf{Rule of thumb:} A predictor with $R^2 = \rho_{Yf}^2$ reduces
required labeled samples by approximately $R^2 \times 100\%$.
\end{tcolorbox}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Practical Calibration from Common Metrics}
\label{sec:calibration}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Practitioners rarely have $\Cov(Y,f)$ directly; instead they report
standard model-validation metrics.  This section describes three
input paths.

\subsection{From MSE and $R^2$ (Continuous)}

For continuous outcomes with a well-calibrated predictor:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\text{MSE} = \E[(Y-f)^2] = \sigma_Y^2(1-\rho_{Yf}^2)$ when
    $f$ is the best linear predictor
  \item $R^2 = 1 - \text{MSE}/\sigma_Y^2 = \rho_{Yf}^2$
\end{itemize}
The sample size reduction factor is $1 - R^2$.

\subsection{From Sensitivity/Specificity (Binary Classifiers)}

For binary $Y \in \{0,1\}$ with prevalence $p$, given
$\mathrm{sens}$ and $\mathrm{spec}$:
\begin{align}
\sigma_f^2 &= p_{\hat Y}(1-p_{\hat Y}), \quad
  p_{\hat Y} = \mathrm{sens}\cdot p + (1-\mathrm{spec})(1-p), \\
\Cov(Y,f) &= \mathrm{sens}\cdot p - p\cdot p_{\hat Y}.
\end{align}

\subsection{From Confusion Matrix (General)}

Given a confusion matrix (TP, FP, FN, TN), the package derives
prevalence $p = (\text{TP}+\text{FN})/m$, prediction prevalence
$p_{\hat Y} = (\text{TP}+\text{FP})/m$, sensitivity
$= \text{TP}/(\text{TP}+\text{FN})$, and specificity
$= \text{TN}/(\text{TN}+\text{FP})$, then applies the formulas above.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Extensions to Other Designs}
\label{sec:extensions}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Binary Outcomes}
\label{sec:binary}

For binary $Y$ with prevalence $p$, $\sigma_Y^2 = p(1-p)$.  The
variance components $\sigma_f^2$ and $\Cov(Y,f)$ are derived from
sensitivity and specificity as in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}.

\subsection{Two-Sample $t$-Test}
\label{sec:ttest}

For comparing groups $A$ and $B$, the PPI++ estimator for
$\Delta^\star = \theta_A^\star - \theta_B^\star$ has variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-ttest-var}
\Var(\wh\Delta_{\lambda^\star}) =
  \frac{\sigma_{Y,A}^2(1-\rho_A^2)}{n_A} +
  \frac{\sigma_{Y,B}^2(1-\rho_B^2)}{n_B} + O(1/N),
\end{equation}
where $\rho_A,\rho_B$ are group-specific correlations.  Under balanced
design ($n_A=n_B=n$) and equal variances:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-ttest-n}
n \ge \frac{2\sigma_Y^2(1 - \bar\rho^2)}
  {(\Delta/(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}))^2},
\quad \bar\rho^2 = (\rho_A^2+\rho_B^2)/2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Paired $t$-Test}
\label{sec:paired}

Let $D_i = Y_i^A - Y_i^B$ and
$f_i^D = f^A(X_i) - f^B(X_i)$.  The problem reduces to mean
estimation on differences.  With
$\rho_D = \Corr(D, f^D)$, the optimal variance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi++-paired-var}
\Var(\wh\Delta_{\lambda^\star}) \approx
  \frac{\sigma_D^2(1-\rho_D^2)}{n},
\end{equation}
and the required sample size is
$n \ge \sigma_D^2(1-\rho_D^2) / S^2$.

\begin{tcolorbox}
\textbf{Note:} For paired designs, $\rho_D$ may differ from
single-outcome correlations; if the predictor captures within-subject
variation well, $\rho_D$ can be high even when marginal correlations are
moderate.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{EIF for Binary Surrogates (LLM-as-a-Judge Calibration)}
\label{sec:eif-binary}

When a binary surrogate (e.g., an LLM judge) produces hard labels
$\hat Y \in \{0,1\}$ on a large test set of size~$N$, and a calibration
set of size $m_{\text{cal}}$ has both $\hat Y$ and human labels $Y$, the
post-stratified EIF estimator is
$\hat\theta_{\mathrm{EIF}} = (1-p_{\hat Y})\hat\mu_0 + p_{\hat Y}\hat\mu_1$,
where $\mu_k = \E[Y \mid \hat Y = k]$.  Its variance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eif-binary-var}
\Var(\hat\theta_{\mathrm{EIF}}) \approx
  \frac{(\mu_1-\mu_0)^2 p_{\hat Y}(1-p_{\hat Y})}{N} +
  \frac{(1-p_{\hat Y})\mu_0(1-\mu_0) + p_{\hat Y}\mu_1(1-\mu_1)}
  {m_{\text{cal}}}.
\end{equation}
Power and sample size formulas follow by substituting this variance into
the standard Wald test framework.

\section{Applications}
\label{sec:applications}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{LLM-as-a-Judge for Binary Preferences}

Pairwise evaluation data follow a measurement-error model: an LLM judge
produces hard labels $\hat Y \in \{0,1\}$ on a large test set ($N$),
and a small calibration set of size $m_{\text{cal}}$ has both $\hat Y$
and human labels~$Y$.  The EIF estimator
(Section~\ref{sec:eif-binary}) applies directly.

\paragraph{Real-data calibration.}
With GPT-4o-mini data ($p \approx 0.236$, sensitivity $\approx 0.25$,
specificity $\approx 0.875$, $p_{\hat Y} \approx 0.16$), detecting a
win-rate shift of $\Delta=0.05$ with $80\%$ power requires
$m_{\text{cal}} \approx 556$, while $m_{\text{cal}}=100$ delivers only
$\approx 0.22$ power.  Setting~9 validates this formula via simulation.

\subsection{Continuous Score Differences}

For continuous outcomes (e.g., quality or toxicity scores), the
two-sample PPI++ test uses variance components from $R^2$.  With
$N=5000$ unlabeled per arm, $\sigma_Y^2 \approx 1$, $\rho \approx 0.7$
($R^2 \approx 0.49$), and $\Delta=0.15$: $n=400$ human labels per arm
achieves $\approx 0.82$ power vs.\ $\approx 0.54$ at $n=200$.

\subsection{Links to IPW and Adaptive Design}

The PPI++ and EIF estimators can be viewed as augmented IPW estimators
with constant inclusion probability.  The power/sample-size routines
are compatible with extensions using stratum-specific variances or
design weights.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We have presented \texttt{PPPower}, a framework for prediction-powered
power and sample size calculation.  The key contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Closed-form power and sample size formulas for PPI++ that reduce
    to classical formulas when $\rho_{Yf} = 0$.
  \item User-friendly parameterization via $R^2$,
    sensitivity/specificity, and confusion matrices.
  \item Simulation-based power estimation for complex settings.
  \item Comprehensive validation across 13~simulation settings, covering
    Type~I error calibration, sample size inversion accuracy, the rule
    of thumb $n_{\text{PPI}}/n_{\text{classical}} \approx 1-R^2$, and
    plug-in $\lambda$ convergence.
  \item An open-source R package implementing all methods.
\end{enumerate}

As AI-powered studies become increasingly common, \texttt{PPPower}
offers a practical tool for planning prediction-powered analyses that
fully exploit modern machine learning predictions.
